The design rule the books keep returning to: torpedoes rule long range, PDCs rule the last few kilometres, and a railgun only hits a dodging ship when it's close or very fast. Δv (reaction mass), not acceleration, decides who can afford to keep fighting.

Movement

The Epstein drive and what it allows

A fusion drive burning water for reaction mass, efficient enough to hold 1 g for weeks. Solomon Epstein's first test (the Drive novella) counted "four gravities… Five. Six. Seven." and ran 37 hours to about 5% of light speed, around 11.5 g on average.

Why canon trips run long

A full flip-and-burn from Ceres to the Ring at 0.3 g takes about 22 days. The books say about three months. So ships burn, coast, then burn, and reaction mass is the real budget.

Fan estimates put exhaust velocity at 1–7% of light speed and the Rocinante's Δv near 4,000 km/s. DERIVED

The Ring and slow zone

  • About 1,000,000 km across, 1,373 gates. BOOK
  • Anything over 600 m/s is stopped dead. It grips the hull, not the people inside. BOOK
  • After weapons fire, the limit dropped to ~40 m/s in ~5 s, about an 11 g shock. DERIVED
  • Too much mass through a gate at once and ships vanish (13 of 437). BOOK
Human limits

The crew is the weakest part of the ship

Drives can outpull people. Combat burns ride on crash couches, gel, and the juice: an anti-blackout injection through the couch that extends g tolerance. It leaves crews wrung out afterwards.

Exotic weapons

  • Drive plume: an Epstein exhaust deals heavy damage astern. BOOK
  • Rock bombardment: 3 stealth-coated rocks, seen about 1 s before impact. No canon mass or speed.
  • Laconian magnetic weapon: one shot kills a ship or station, and firing it stuns the whole system for ~3 min. The "<3,000,000 km" range is a wiki figure. WIKI
  • Jamming: under 3 s of blindness was enough for 5 torpedo hits. BOOK

What kills ships

  1. Reactor containment failure. Dumping core stops it but kills the drive.
  2. Torpedoes that saturate the PDC screen.
  3. Railgun hits through vital systems.
  4. Boarding.
  5. Sensor blind spots. That's how the Heart of the Tempest died.

Sensors & stealth

There is almost nowhere to hide

  • A lit drive is visible across the whole system; real-physics estimates say 0.5–80 AU. Stealth works only with the drive cold. BOOK REAL
  • Cold hull at room temperature: real telescopes could find it at 2–60 million km. For the sim, use 10,000–100,000 km for a bare hull and 1,000 km or less for a stealth-coated one. DERIVED
  • Canon stealth: Protogen black composite with active cooling, stealth-coated Free Navy rocks, the Rocinante's disguise kit. BOOK
  • One light-second is ~300,000 km. Inside torpedo range the lag is under a second; between planets it is minutes, so you see where the enemy was. REAL
  • Lighting the drive or firing any weapon reveals you for that turn and the next. DERIVED
  • RPG stealth: −2 to enemy detection, −3 at long range; the coating fails after 3 hull losses. RPG
Recommendation

Simulator model

SystemRecommendation
PhysicsNewtonian, SI units, 64-bit floats, one local inertial frame per engagement. Fixed timestep with symplectic Euler or velocity Verlet. Skip gravity, relativity, and drive waste heat inside a fight.
Fast roundsTrace railgun and PDC rounds as rays each tick instead of stepping them as particles.
Railgun hitsProbability from target lateral acceleration × flight time², not a hard range. Apply recoil Δv to the shooter.
ResourcesReaction mass (Δv), PDC rounds, torpedo magazine. Coasting torpedoes get easier to kill.
CrewA g-dose meter (g × time). Blackout and injury risk climb steeply above 5 g, scaled by age and juice. Juice buys tolerance at a toxicity cost.
SensorsContacts timestamped with light lag. A lit drive is always detected. Firing breaks stealth.
DamageHull soak plus typed system losses: reactor, drive, sensors, thrusters, each weapon, crew. Reactor hits can escalate to containment failure.
PresetsBook: railgun 1,500 km/s, 25 g PDC rounds. Show: railgun 10 km/s, 40 mm PDC rounds.
